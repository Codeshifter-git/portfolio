Inside the edit

One video, four decisions. This is the work behind the 148K plays — nothing here is a template or an auto-cut.

A Clip selection

Hours of Mr Robot footage watched down to the few seconds that carry the character. Every clip in the final 52 seconds earned its place — if a shot didn't raise the tension, it was cut, however good it looked on its own.

B Beat sync

Cuts land on the track, not near it. Hits, drops and dialogue beats are marked first, then the picture is built around them — so the edit feels inevitable instead of assembled.

C Transitions & pacing

Fast where the scene is frantic, held where it needs to breathe. Transitions are chosen per cut — whip, match, hard cut — to keep momentum without turning the video into an effects reel.

D Grade & finish

A cold, high-contrast finish that matches Mr Robot's world, checked on a phone screen — because that's where the audience actually watches it.

  1. Watch everything first

    I go through all the source footage before touching a timeline, noting the moments with real energy. Most editors skip this; it's where good edits actually come from.

  2. Mark the beat, build the hook

    The track's beats get marked, the strongest three seconds go first, and the structure is built backwards from what will make someone stop scrolling.

  3. Cut, then cut again

    First pass for story, second for rhythm, third for transitions and grade. Then a phone check — if it doesn't hold on a small screen, it isn't finished.

  4. Judge it on your footage

    I've cut for clients as well as for my own channel — and the fastest way to judge fit is still fresh work. Send a raw clip and I'll cut a short trial edit, so you can judge how I work with your material directly.
